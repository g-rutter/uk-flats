# UK One-Bed Flat Location Research Plan

## 1. Aim and scope

Compare the 63 supplied locations in England and Wales for:

- buying a one-bedroom flat; and
- renting a one-bedroom flat.

Buying and renting receive separate evidence, scores and grades. The project ranks **locations and promising subareas**, not individual properties. Tower blocks are preferred and credible recurring tower/high-rise stock is required for the highest grades.

The priority factors are affordability, personal safety, car-free transport, pleasant local condition and quiet surroundings. National transport and market thickness are secondary factors.

### Boundaries

- Broadly screen all 63 locations; deeply assess only the strongest 15–20.
- Examine at most 2–4 promising subareas per shortlisted location.
- Target stock is one-bedroom flats only. Broader flat data may be used only as a clearly labelled screening proxy.
- Exclude Scotland, Northern Ireland, houses, studios, two-bedroom stock, growth forecasts and individual-property recommendations.
- Do not perform exhaustive neighbourhood searches, street-by-street noise modelling or full building, cladding, fire-safety, leasehold or legal due diligence.
- Flag conspicuous service-charge, lease or building risks only when they materially affect an area-level conclusion.

## 2. Research stages

### Stage 0 — Framework

Create the canonical location/geography table, fix common data periods and establish an evidence log containing source, retrieval date, geography, observation count and limitations. Local-authority evidence must not be presented as neighbourhood evidence.

### Stage 1 — Cheap broad screen of all 63

This stage uses only data that are readily available and scalable across the entire list. It must **not** estimate one-bedroom purchase prices manually, collect samples of individual listings, deduplicate properties, identify particular tower blocks or research subareas in detail.

For each location:

1. **Safety:** use recent violent/sexual-crime and antisocial-behaviour evidence over a common 12-month period. Prefer rates with matched boundaries and denominators; otherwise label the result as a relative indicator.
2. **Buying affordability:** use a recent large-sample achieved-price measure for flats of all sizes, normally a 24-month HM Land Registry median. Label it explicitly as an all-flat proxy and show transaction count. Do not call it a one-bedroom median.
3. **Renting affordability:** use an official one-bedroom rent median when directly available and adequately sampled; otherwise use a clearly labelled broader rental proxy. Do not construct a manual listing sample.
4. **Market thickness:** use search-result counts or other readily available summary indicators for one-bedroom stock. Do not open and code individual listings. Treat counts as snapshots. Record only a coarse known/likely/unclear indication of tower stock.
5. **Local transport:** make a coarse car-free assessment using bus, rail and tram/metro availability.
6. **National transport:** record representative fastest reasonable weekday rail times to London and Birmingham, including changes; ignore impractical one-off services.
7. **Noise and condition:** use quick, coarse evidence on major roads, railways, airports, industry, nightlife, dereliction and general upkeep. Do not undertake detailed visual surveys.

Stage 1 produces screening proxies, confidence labels and a shortlist—not definitive one-bedroom prices or neighbourhood findings.

### Stage 2 — Choose 15–20 locations

Score buying and renting separately, then shortlist the union of the strongest candidates. Begin with the top 12 on each track. If their union exceeds 20, retain the strongest priority-factor performers with the clearest evidence; if it is below 15, add the next strongest.

| Screening factor | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Affordability | 15 |
| Personal safety | 15 |
| Car-free local transport | 15 |
| Pleasant local condition | 15 |
| Quiet surroundings | 15 |
| One-bed/tower stock | 15 |
| National transport | 10 |

Use a small ordinal scale with confidence flags. The screening score selects candidates; it is not the final letter grade. A location strong for only one tenure must remain eligible on that track.

### Stage 3 — Detailed work on the shortlist

For each shortlisted location:

1. Identify 2–4 plausible subareas that best combine quiet, condition, safety, transport and apartment/tower stock.
2. Refine crime evidence using consistent small-area geographies and the same 12-month period.
3. Estimate actual one-bedroom buying and renting levels from adequately sized evidence sets, reporting asking versus achieved values, date window, geography, range, median, sample size and confidence.
4. Deduplicate obvious cross-listings and separate tower/high-rise stock from low-rise purpose-built flats and conversions.
5. Judge whether suitable stock is recurring or consists of isolated outliers.
6. Conduct limited visual, noise and transport checks for the candidate subareas rather than every street.
7. Record material area-level caveats, including conspicuous service charges, short leases or problematic blocks.

### Stage 4 — Grade, validate and build

Check dates, boundaries, units, duplicates, missing values and outliers; manually review every proposed A+/A result. Generate separate buy/rent grades and validate the interactive display against the research dataset.

## 3. Price and rent rules

### Buying

HM Land Registry Price Paid Data provide robust achieved sales and property type but not bedroom count. Therefore:

- Stage 1 uses the 24-month all-flat median only as a screening proxy.
- One-bedroom estimation begins only in Stage 3.
- Display a median only with at least 10 distinct one-bedroom observations; prefer 20+. Below 10, show a range and low-confidence label.
- Keep achieved, asking and auction/guide prices separate.
- Exclude or flag shared ownership, retirement property, non-standard sales, cash-only/auction anomalies and obvious duplicates when distorting the market.

### Renting

- Stage 1 uses an official one-bedroom median where directly available, otherwise a broad proxy.
- Current one-bedroom asking-rent sampling begins only in Stage 3.
- Keep achieved and advertised rents separate. Existing-tenancy evidence may lag the market faced by a new tenant.
- Apply the same 10-observation minimum for a median and convert weekly rents to calendar-month equivalents.

### Relative affordability

There is no personal price ceiling. Compare locations separately within the buying and renting distributions:

- cheapest third: above average;
- middle third: around average;
- most expensive third: below average.

Price alone cannot override serious safety, transport or condition weaknesses.

## 4. Final categories and grades

The seven final categories are affordability, safety, market thickness, quiet, local transport, national transport and local condition.

| Category | Above-average test |
| --- | --- |
| Affordability | Favourable third for that tenure, supported by adequate evidence |
| Safety | Better than the comparison norm on violent/sexual crime and ASB, without a serious subarea warning |
| Market thickness | Repeatable one-bedroom choice rather than isolated listings |
| Quiet | A plausible subarea avoids major persistent noise without losing basic accessibility |
| Local transport | Daily life is credibly practical without a car |
| National transport | A representative journey is under two hours to London, Birmingham or both |
| Local condition | A plausible subarea appears broadly pleasant and cared-for rather than persistently neglected |

| Grade | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| A+ | Above average in all 7 categories |
| A | Above average in 6 of 7 |
| B | Above average in 5 of 7 |
| C | Above average in 4 of 7 |
| D | Above average in 3 of 7 |
| E | Above average in 0–2 of 7 |

Safeguards:

- A+ and A additionally require credible recurring tower/high-rise stock and no serious priority-factor weakness.
- A+ requires all five priority factors to be above average and no low-confidence critical category.
- Unknown does not count as above average; a critical unknown adds a low-confidence marker.
- Every qualitative outcome needs a short reason, not a spurious decimal score.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

1. Official data: HM Land Registry; ONS/Welsh Government rent data; data.police.uk; Census/geographic data; official transport timetables.
2. Large property portals and sold-listing archives for shortlist-only bedroom and market evidence.
3. Local authority planning, regeneration, noise and transport material.
4. Limited street-level imagery and reputable local reporting.
5. Estate-agent or crowd-sourced claims only as corroboration.

Retain a source link/citation, data period, retrieval date, geography and sample size for each displayed value where applicable. Clearly distinguish facts, estimates and judgements.

## 6. Output

Deliver two user-facing files:

1. `index.html` — layout, styles and application logic.
2. `locations.js` — structured data as a JavaScript constant, allowing direct local opening without `file://` JSON-fetch problems.

The interface will have:

- an embedded, attributed SVG map of Great Britain on the left;
- grade letters on markers and location name/headline value on hover or keyboard focus;
- a buy/rent toggle changing marker grades and tenure-specific data;
- a right-hand detail panel showing both buying and renting evidence;
- filters for grade, shortlist status, tower availability and confidence;
- candidate subareas, strengths, weaknesses, caveats, evidence dates and sources;
- an accessible mobile layout, with colour never the sole grade indicator.

Each location record should contain identity/coordinates, shortlist status, separate buy/rent objects, prices/rents and sample metadata, one-bed/tower evidence, category outcomes, grades, subareas, journey times, summaries, caveats, sources and research dates.

## 7. Stopping, QA and handover rules

- Stop Stage 1 when all 63 locations have comparable screening proxies; do not expand it into listing or neighbourhood research.
- Stop Stage 3 at 2–4 credible subareas per shortlisted location and once price evidence meets the threshold or is explicitly marked insufficient.
- Never infer tower stock merely from the word “apartment”; high grades require named-building, floor, imagery or equivalent evidence.
- Mark missing evidence unknown rather than silently good or bad.
- Any major methodology expansion requires an explicit scope decision.

Preserve one resumable checkpoint after each stage:

- Stage 0: geography table, rubric and evidence structure.
- Stage 1: complete 63-location screen and evidence log.
- Stage 2: separate buy/rent rankings and accepted shortlist.
- Stage 3: detailed shortlist records.
- Stage 4: validated `index.html` and `locations.js`.

Each checkpoint needs only a short handover note covering completed work, unresolved issues, source dates and the next action. Accepted earlier stages should not be rerun without a specific identified defect.

## 8. Execution sequence

1. Freeze this plan.
2. Complete Stages 0–1.
3. Review and approve the 15–20-location shortlist.
4. Complete Stage 3 in small batches.
5. Grade each tenure separately.
6. Build and validate the two interactive files.

The shortlist approval is the main cost-control gate: detailed listing, tower, subarea and visual research must not spread back across all 63 locations.
